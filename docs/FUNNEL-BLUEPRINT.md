# The funnel logic
The homepage is a direct-response sales funnel wrapped in a Pacific Northwest brand — not an agency portfolio. Every section moves the visitor toward one action: starting the $799 30-Day Program. Because most visitors aren’t ready on visit one, the page also offers a lower-commitment path — the free presence check — that captures the rest and feeds follow-up.
Visitor journey:  Understand the offer → recognize the problem → feel the cost of doing nothing → see the complete solution → understand the value → trust the guarantee → see proof → self-identify (“this is for me”) → remove objections → act.
Primary goal:  Apply for the 30-Day Program.
Secondary (soft) goal:  Get a free presence check — captures the ~90% not ready to buy, and builds a warm follow-up list.

# Section blueprint

## 01 ·  Header
Purpose:  Orient the visitor and keep the primary action one tap away — without competing with the funnel.
Contains:  Official horizontal logo (left); nav links How It Works, What’s Included, Results, About; EN/ES toggle; Apply Now button in Oregon Rust.
Behavior:  Clean sticky header after scroll begins; nav links anchor to sections; CTA always visible; mobile collapses to a drawer with Apply prominent.
Why:  A minimal header signals focus. Extra destinations (Blog, Services, Resources) leak attention out of the funnel — leave them off at launch.

## 02 ·  Hero — sell the outcome
Purpose:  In seconds, answer what you do, who it’s for, and why they should care — and present the primary action.
Contains, top to bottom:  eyebrow → headline → subhead → primary CTA + secondary CTA → soft audit CTA → trust line, with the valley illustration bleeding behind the composition.
Approved copy:
Eyebrow: WEB DESIGN · LOCAL SEO · LOCAL GROWTH
Headline: Websites That Grow Local Businesses.  (“Local Businesses.” set in rust)
Subhead: We build your website and your whole online presence — from Google and local SEO to the systems that turn clicks into booked jobs.
Primary CTA: Apply for the 30-Day Program →
Secondary CTA: See how it works ↓
Soft CTA: Not ready yet? Get a free presence check →
Trust line: Based in Oregon · English & Español
Why:  Sells growth, not “$799 websites” — price comes later. The three-tier CTA stack (apply / learn / free check) gives every visitor a next step by readiness. The illustration carries Oregon character while the text stays fully readable on cream.

## 03 ·  Problem — a website isn’t enough
Purpose:  Reframe the company from “website builder” to “local digital infrastructure partner,” and make the visitor feel the gap.
Contains:  Headline “A website isn’t enough.”; short message; a connected system diagram (Website + Google + SEO + Listings + Social); closing line “We connect the whole system.”
Why:  If customers can’t find you, the site can’t do its job. Naming “disconnected presence” as the real problem sets up the full scope of the offer that follows.

## 04 ·  Cost of inaction  (recommendation)
Purpose:  Loss aversion — make staying invisible feel expensive, so “I’ll do it later” carries a cost.
Contains:  A short, punchy dark band. Draft copy: “Every day an invisible business loses jobs to the competitor who shows up first on Google. The site you’ve been meaning to fix is quietly sending customers somewhere else. We fix that in 30 days.”
Why:  Benefit copy alone underperforms for people who’ve procrastinated on their site for a year. One loss-aversion beat measurably lifts urgency.

## 05 ·  What’s included — complete local presence
Purpose:  Establish scope so $799 feels substantial before the price is ever shown.
Contains:  Headline “Your entire local presence. Built for you.”; supporting copy; a grid of eight deliverable cards.
Deliverables:
Custom Website — responsive, conversion-focused business site.
Local SEO Foundation — titles, metadata, local structure, core on-page setup.
Google Business Optimization — profile setup or optimization.
Local Listings & Citations — Yelp, Nextdoor, relevant directories.
Social Presence Setup — relevant business profiles.
Lead Capture & Forms — calls, forms, quote pathways.
Analytics & Tracking — visibility into traffic and conversions.
Ongoing Support & Management — transitions into the $99/mo service after launch.
Why:  Listing the connected pieces makes the offer read as infrastructure, not a template. Keep the cards restrained and on-brand.

## 06 ·  The Willamette 30-Day Program
Purpose:  Turn a vague service into a named product with a clear, finite timeline.
Contains:  A retro poster badge — “The Willamette 30-Day Program” — paired with a process timeline.
Days 1–3 · Build — gather info, build the site, assemble core systems.
Days 4–7 · Launch — review, publish, connect the local presence.
Days 8–30 · Optimize — monitor, refine, support the live system.
Day 30 · Review — review the experience, deliverables, and next steps together.
Positioning guardrail:  Never imply a guaranteed number of leads. The risk reversal is satisfaction-based, not an outcome promise.
Why:  “We do the work first, then you decide” lowers perceived risk and justifies the application framing.

## 07 ·  Guarantee — primary risk reversal
Purpose:  Neutralize the “why pay $799 upfront” objection right where it arises.
Contains:  Headline “30-Day Money-Back Satisfaction Guarantee”; plain-language explanation; a 30-day seal; the upfront-risk bridge line: “You’re protected — so paying upfront carries the risk, not you.”
PLACEHOLDER  Final terms — start date, eligibility, request process, exclusions, hard-cost treatment, and asset ownership after a refund — must match the signed agreement before launch.
Why:  Placing the guarantee directly beside the price is what makes upfront payment feel safe.

## 08 ·  Value stack + pricing  (recommendation)
Purpose:  Anchor the value high, then reveal $799 so it reads as a steal rather than an expense.
Value stack (illustrative):
Custom business website (freelance) — $1,800+
Local SEO foundation & setup — $900+
Google Business + listings & citations — $600+
Lead capture, analytics & tracking — $500+
Typical separate cost — $3,800+   →   Willamette: $799
Then two cards:  $799 One-Time Launch (included deliverables + guarantee reminder) and $99/month Ongoing Management (hosting, updates, support, monitoring, optimization).
Transparency:  Disclose any hard/third-party costs; state when $99/mo begins; state whether it’s optional or required; never hide material terms behind the CTA.
PLACEHOLDER  Confirm stack values, hard costs, and billing terms against approved business terms before launch.
Why:  A reader who has already mentally totaled $3,800+ sees $799 as value, not cost.

## 09 ·  Results / proof  (recommendation)
Purpose:  Answer “can they help a business like mine?” without fabricating data.
At launch, before verified metrics exist:  use a founder video (even a 60-second Loom lifts local conversion), real deliverables and a dashboard screenshot, Google reviews as they arrive, and process / “here’s exactly what you’ll get” case studies.
Card anatomy (once real cases exist):  project visual, business name + Oregon city, scope, short before/after, one or two verified metrics.
PLACEHOLDER  Never publish invented percentages or the concept-mockup placeholder businesses as real claims.
Why:  Trust is the gate to the sale; several honest proof types fill the gap a portfolio normally would.

## 10 ·  Local / Oregon trust  (recommendation)
Purpose:  Turn regional identity into positioning, and let the right visitors self-identify.
Contains:  Headline “Built for Oregon businesses — en inglés y en español.”; Willamette Valley roots; service areas (Albany, Corvallis, Salem, Lebanon + surrounding); the bilingual promise as a wedge, not a footnote; heritage badge or valley illustration.
Why:  “The only local team that builds and runs your whole presence in English and Spanish” is a genuine differentiator for an underserved market.

## 11 ·  Scarcity  (recommendation)
Purpose:  Honest urgency that counters “I’ll do it later.”
Contains:  A slim strip: “We take on a limited number of new businesses each month to keep every launch personal.”
Why:  A real capacity limit — true, and it reinforces the selective “program” framing.

## 12 ·  FAQ — objection handling
Purpose:  Answer the objections that surface in a sales conversation, before the final ask.
Questions (the first two are additions):
How much of my time does this actually take?
Do I need to be technical?
Do I own my website?
What exactly does the $799 include?
How does the 30-day money-back satisfaction guarantee work?
Are any hard costs non-refundable?
What happens after the first 30 days? / What does the $99/month include?
Can you replace or redesign my existing website?
Can you help if I don’t have a Google Business Profile?
Do you work with businesses outside Albany?
Do you offer service in Spanish?
PLACEHOLDER  Answers touching the guarantee, pricing, and ownership must match approved terms.
Why:  Clearing friction right before the CTA lifts completion.

## 13 ·  Free presence check — soft conversion  (recommendation)
Purpose:  Capture the ~90% not ready to apply, demonstrate expertise, and build a warm follow-up list. This is the single highest-leverage addition.
Contains:  Headline “See what’s missing — free.”; what it checks (Google Business Profile status, mobile speed, listings & citation gaps, where competitors outrank them); a short form (business name, website, email); reassurance microcopy.
Follow-up:  This only pays off if follow-up emails are sequenced behind it — most local leads convert on the third or fourth touch. Productize the audit so it stays semi-automated and doesn’t eat capacity.
Why:  Turns otherwise-lost visitors into leads at the top of a nurture path.

## 14 ·  Final CTA — close
Purpose:  One last, visually strong, singular ask.
Contains:  Headline “Let’s build something that brings you business.”; primary CTA “Apply for the 30-Day Program →”; friction-reducer “Takes about 2 minutes”; dark forest background, cream type, rust CTA.
Why:  A clean, single close once objections are handled.

## 15 ·  Footer
Purpose:  Useful, restrained navigation and legal.
Contains:  Horizontal logo; short description (Oregon web design & local growth); service-area statement; nav links; EN/ES; Privacy Policy and Terms of Service; © 2026 Willamette Web Design; optional ESTD 2026 detail.
PLACEHOLDER  Contact email and phone are TBD — do not ship placeholder contact information from the mockup.

# The application flow  (after Apply)
The primary CTA opens a short qualification flow that feels like applying to a program — not a generic contact form. Recommended: shorten Step 1 to the essentials and make later fields optional, or A/B test the full four steps against a two-field “start here.” Always show a progress indicator so it never feels endless.
Step 1 · Business — name, industry/service, city/service area, current website URL.
Step 2 · Current presence — Google Business Profile? active platforms? biggest problem?
Step 3 · Goals — primary service to grow, desired customer, typical job value, primary goal.
Step 4 · Contact — name, phone, email, preferred language.
Completion: a confirmation page that explains exactly what happens next, with a booking step if a discovery call is part of the process.

# Design & brand — quick reference
Cream #F3E7CE dominant background; Forest #233D32 typography and dark sections; Rust #D5683C reserved for CTAs; River Blue, Valley Sage, Harvest Gold support illustration and detail.
Retro illustration for character; the interface itself stays clean, fast, and scannable.
Avoid generic SaaS styling, purple gradients, glassmorphism, neon, and stock laptop imagery.
One primary action per section; rust is the conversion color and nothing else.

# Technical guardrails
Semantic HTML, accessible heading order, WCAG-conscious contrast, keyboard navigation, prefers-reduced-motion.
Responsive image formats, lazy-load below the fold, optimize for Core Web Vitals and real cellular connections.
Track: CTA clicks, application starts and completions, phone clicks, form conversions, and audit submissions.
Build the bilingual structure cleanly so Spanish is never an afterthought.
No hard-coded placeholder metrics, phone, or email. Guarantee and pricing copy must come from approved terms before deploy.
