Homepage Funnel — Recommendations & Improvement Opportunities
Consultant review of the Homepage Funnel Structure & Developer Guide
Prepared for internal review
The current brief is strong. The funnel logic is sound, the CTA discipline is disciplined, and the "sell the outcome, reveal price later" sequencing is exactly right. The recommendations below aren't about fixing what's broken — they're about closing the gaps where we're likely leaving leads on the table. They're ordered roughly by impact.

# Priority Recommendations

## 1.  Add a soft-conversion path for the 90% who aren't ready to apply
HIGHEST IMPACT
Every CTA currently points to one action: apply. But most first-time visitors to a $799 offer won't commit on visit one — and we give them nothing else to do, so they leave and we never hear from them.
The classic wedge for local SEO and web is a free local visibility audit: "See exactly what's broken in your online presence — free." It captures an email, demonstrates expertise, and builds a warm follow-up list. It also doubles as a qualification tool. For a small shop, productize it (Google Business Profile check, mobile speed, listings and citation gaps, review count) so it's semi-automated and doesn't eat capacity.

## 2.  Stack and anchor the value before revealing $799
HIGH IMPACT
The guide says to make the offer feel substantial, but it doesn't explicitly price-anchor. Before the number lands, spell out what these pieces cost separately — a freelance designer, an SEO consultant, Google and listings setup — which can easily run several thousand dollars.
When the reader has already mentally added it up, $799 reads as a steal instead of an expense. Pair it with a short comparison against the real alternatives they're weighing: DIY Wix/Squarespace, a cheap freelancer, or doing nothing.

## 3.  Resolve the "why pay upfront" tension directly
HIGH IMPACT
There's a subtle friction: the program says "we do the work first, then you decide," but we collect $799 before they see anything. The guarantee bridges this, but the bridge should be explicit near the price — one line such as "You're protected by the 30-day guarantee, so paying upfront carries the risk, not you."
Optionally, test a deposit-plus-on-launch structure. It can lift conversion for cash-sensitive small businesses, though it complicates cash flow, so weigh that trade-off.

## 4.  Sharpen who this is for
HIGH IMPACT
"Local businesses" is broad, and broad converts worse than specific — people apply when they think this is for me. Two options:
Name the trades we serve best — cleaners, dentists, landscapers, contractors.
Lean into the bilingual angle as positioning, not a footnote. Spanish-speaking Oregon business owners are badly underserved by web designers. "The only Oregon web team that builds and runs your presence in English and Español" is a genuine wedge, not a feature bullet.

## 5.  Use real scarcity
MEDIUM IMPACT · QUICK WIN
A small shop has real capacity limits — turn that into honest urgency: "We take on a limited number of new businesses each month." It's true, it justifies the application framing, and it counters the "I'll do it later" delay that kills local leads.

## 6.  Add a cost-of-inaction beat
MEDIUM IMPACT
The funnel sells what customers gain; it doesn't dramatize what staying invisible costs — the competitor showing up first, the calls that go elsewhere. A short loss-aversion moment near the problem section or before the final CTA tends to outperform pure benefit copy for people who've been "meaning to fix their site" for a year.

## 7.  Reconsider the four-step application
MEDIUM IMPACT · NEEDS TESTING
Four steps is a lot of friction for a cold visitor. There's a real strategic fork: the length creates commitment and a "selective program" feel (good), but it also loses impatient leads (bad).
Either shorten step one to the bare minimum (name, business, phone) and make the rest optional, or A/B test the full application against a two-field "start here" version. Whatever we choose, show a progress indicator so it never feels endless.

## 8.  Build a proof strategy for a business with no portfolio yet
MEDIUM IMPACT · LAUNCH-CRITICAL
The guide rightly forbids fake metrics — but that leaves the results section empty at launch. Fill the trust gap other ways:
A founder video — even a 60-second Loom dramatically lifts local conversion.
The actual deliverables and a real dashboard screenshot.
Google reviews as they come in.
Process and "here's exactly what you'll get" case studies until real numbers exist.

# Two Smaller Notes
Follow-up / nurture is missing. Most local leads convert on the third or fourth touch, so the audit email capture in recommendation 1 only pays off if we actually sequence follow-ups behind it.
The FAQ has two gaps. It's missing the two objections busy owners raise most: "how much of my time does this take?" and "do I need to be technical?"

# Suggested Next Step
Any one of these can be turned into drop-in section copy and layout for the build. The three highest-leverage starting points are the audit wedge (recommendation 1), the value-stack copy (recommendation 2), or a reworked application flow (recommendation 7).
